01

The one idea worth keeping

A dated room is the answer to the failure mode this product's own framework names as its most likely killer.

The framework is candid about where this dies: most users' complete experience is that they entered someone and heard nothing, forever. Marriage Pact beats that by guaranteeing a payoff — fill in the survey, receive a name. Celestual has never had one, and every mitigation on offer is a consolation rather than a cure.

A room fixes it exactly. Trace what changes for the person writing.

 Before the roomWith the room
What writing buysa chance, of unknown size, at an unknown timea named person invited to a place, on a date, where you will be
When you find outpossibly nevera date you can count down to
What you can tell a friendnothing, without confessing“the thing on the 8th”
Whether the act is socialprivate, and slightly lonelyan act inside a shared campus event

That is the Marriage Pact property, obtained without a survey and without promising anybody a match. It converts the product's worst structural feature — that the outcome is probabilistic — into a scheduling problem, which is a solvable kind of problem.

The room is not a side business. It is the missing half of the product, and it should be funded and measured as product.

02

The arithmetic that kills the hour

The 01:00 unlock is the reason people are supposed to come. It does not work, and the reason is arithmetic rather than execution.

Take a room of n people. Each writes m letters aimed at someone in the room. Write r for the real chance that the person you wrote about independently wrote about you — the assortative rate, well above the random baseline, because people write about people they are actually drawn to.

Expected mutual pairs ≈ (n · m · r) / 2, and under pure chance it collapses to m² / 2 — independent of room size. A bigger room does not help. Only more letters do.

The two fixes, both required

Three letters at the door, aimed at the room. Going from one to three moves chance-only pairs from 0.5 to 4.6 and — the number that matters more — takes the share of the room receiving nothing from 37% to 5%. The composer must suggest from the people actually present: a letter about somebody who is not in the building cannot resolve tonight.

Stop making the mutual the payoff. Even at three letters, the reveal fires for a minority, and a ceremony whose payoff reaches a minority teaches the majority they were extras in somebody else's evening.

The count, then the reveal. Every phone lights at the same second with one true sentence — tonight, N people in this room wrote about you — which fires for nine in ten. Then the pairs resolve.

A guaranteed floor and a rare ceiling. It is the Marriage Pact structure exactly, and it is the difference between a ceremony and a raffle.

Why it passes the wall's own gates

Gate 2 — silence in, silence out — forbids telling a specific person that something was done about them. Three things make the count pass, and all three must hold: the information is already theirs, since anyone can search their own handle; it is a count and never a name; and it is consented at the door, because writing is opting in. The count fires only for people who wrote that night. No exceptions, enforced in the data model rather than in the run of show.

04

Five things that break it

1 · Sixty percent of the population cannot enter the venue

A California Type 48 licence — the ordinary bar and nightclub licence — bars anyone under 21 from the premises, not merely from drinking. A Type 47, held by a bona fide eating place, admits them. Sixty percent of Berkeley's 33,122 undergraduates are 18–21, and the manual's own sponsor email says average age 20. An average of 20 in a room that requires 21 is a contradiction sitting in the pitch.

The second half is worse. A bar pays per head because it makes the money back at the bar. A crowd that cannot legally buy a drink generates no bar revenue, so there is nothing to pay you out of. Venues that run 18+ nights profitably do it on the cover charge instead — which makes an 18+ deal a door split, never a per-head payment.

Leg one · 20:00–22:45 · all ages
The product

The wall, the writing, the projection, the count, the reveals, the film. A room you rent or partner into, with no licence problem. The whole population is eligible.

Leg two · 23:00–close · 21+
The money

The subset that can drink walks five minutes to a bar on a per-head deal. They arrive as one group, pre-warmed, at the hour a Tuesday bar is dying — which is more attractive to a bar than a trickle.

Night one runs leg one only. The bar leg is added at night two, once there is a head count to sell.

2 · The invitation does not arrive

The manual's highest-leverage step delivers its message into a folder with no doorbell. A DM from someone you do not follow lands in Message Requests, and Instagram sends no push notification — it sits silently until the person happens to look, and anything read as bulk is routed one level deeper into Hidden Requests. Safe cold-DM throughput is 10–50 a day on an established account; eighty each over three days is right at the edge where action blocks start.

Then the last leg: free events lose 28–50% of confirmed RSVPs to no-shows, at roughly 1.5× the rate of paid ones.

  • Follow first, DM second. A DM from a follower lands in the primary inbox with a notification. This one change is worth more than doubling the DM count.
  • Prefer the reachable. Named people who already hold a verified address are reachable by mail, which arrives. Rank the list by that.
  • Make the wall do the reaching. On a dense campus, “go search your @ on that thing” travels faster than 400 DMs and costs nothing. Word of mouth is the reach channel; the DM is the closer.
  • Cap it honestly. Forty each over five days, from accounts six months old, never from the brand. Five ambassadors reaching 200 well beats 400 badly.

3 · The wall of faces turns commercial the day there is a cover charge

The hive draws one disc per person written to, carrying the profile picture the resolver scraped, of people who consented to none of it. Today that is defensible: a public wall of anonymous letters is expressive content. A cover charge changes its character. California Civil Code §3344 creates liability for knowingly using a readily identifiable person's photograph for purposes of advertising or soliciting purchases of services, at $750 statutory damages per plaintiff plus fees — with several hundred identifiable plaintiffs available.

The faces come off the field before the first ticketed night, and nothing commercial ever appears on the wall. Not the sponsor, not the ticket, not the price, not the date.

Both are cheap. The hive already renders a monogram whenever the resolver has no picture, so the first is a flag rather than a rewrite, and the field's whole design is a function of distance from the light — untouched by what sits inside a disc. The second widens a rule the repo already holds for the sponsor. Keeping the wall expressive is what keeps it protected, and it is the same rule the design already wanted for its own reasons.

4 · The campaign as written cannot legally run

Berkeley campus regulations: nothing may be left unattended on campus grounds or in classrooms, nothing attached to doors or buildings, flyers on bulletin boards and kiosks only, chalking is defacement, and a table must be staffed by a member of the sponsoring organisation. Cafeteria tables, library carrels and under dorm doors — the manual's three placements — are, in order: unattended on campus grounds, unattended on campus grounds, and affixed to doors.

The obvious remedy is closed. Becoming a registered student organisation is a semester-long programme with deadlines of 1 July or 1 December. There is no path to RSO status this autumn — and RSO status is what carries Sproul tabling, room reservation, free on-campus event insurance, and the right to post flyers at all.

Partner with an RSO that already exists. Berkeley has over a thousand; one wants a free event with a film crew. They book, table and post. You bring the product, the campaign and the money.

It converts every blocked row above into a routine one, and it is available this week. Everything else runs off campus property: Telegraph, Bancroft and Durant are city right-of-way, and a card handed to a person beats a card left on a surface anyway.

5 · The cost model is missing most of the costs

Three whole categories are absent from the manual's $875. Insurance, without which no venue confirms a booking — typically $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate with the venue named, at $250–400 dry. The campaign: twenty seed posters at $50–200 appears in the manual's own section 3 and in none of its P&Ls, and neither do the cards, the wall, or the ambassadors who are asked for its highest-leverage labour. And rent, which only a bar absorbs.

Why nothing inside the app is for sale

The $7 reply charges the person who was written about, whom this product's own persona document calls “not a customer — a stakeholder with veto power over the product's existence.” Beyond the constitution, the decisive objection is structural:

It inverts the safety incentive. Revenue would scale with the number of people written about without their consent — and every one of the wall's nine gates would then stand against the direction the money pulls.

A cover charge has the opposite property: it pays you for people who chose to come, which is exactly why it is safe to take. Three more, briefly: it taxes the emotional payoff, which is the moment somebody tells their friends; a better version is already shipped and free; and the mechanism does not hold, because an uncaptured Stripe hold is cancelled seven days after creation, which is the manual's refund window with no margin at all.

The $2 notification is NGL Pro's structure with the deception removed — a payment taken at the most emotionally invested moment for an outcome that may never arrive. Ours would involve no fake messages, no false reveal and no hidden renewals, and it would still be the same shape, sold to the same demographic, by a product whose only real moat is that it can be trusted.

Worth knowing where the category stands: Marriage Pact has 600,000 students across 109 schools, a $5M seed from Bain Capital Ventures, and no revenue. The closest comparable that exists has not found a way to charge students and has decided not to force it.

06

What to build

Five things. Two tables, three screens, none more than a week — and the first is the strongest growth object this product has ever had.

The Named Card

Not in the manual at all. The framework's existing share object asks somebody to make a claim about themselves. The wall produces something far stronger, because it is a fact conferred by somebody else: a person who searches their handle and finds a letter has just received the most flattering piece of information a person can receive. Give them a card that says so.

somebody at berkeley wrote about you.

you're on the list for the 8th. +1.

celestual.us

Never who. Never a word of the letter. Only the fact, which is already theirs, and the door. It is receiver-side by construction, so it confesses nothing; it is a flex that is true and unearned by the poster, which is the strongest social currency available; and it is aimed — it reaches the poster's Berkeley followers, who go and search their own handle, and most find nothing, which routes them to the free notification, which is the front door.

It passes all nine gates. The named attack is a card that says “someone wrote to @them”; this says someone wrote about me, posted by me, carrying no third-party handle, no count about anyone else and nothing a takedown must reach.

The night pass, and three letters

One table, modelled on the pass list that already exists — a short code handed out at the door that grants write access for that night, on that campus, with its own allowance of three letters separate from the rolling window. The door has already verified them by a better method than email: a human looked at them and let them in.

  • Aim at the room. The composer suggests from the night's attendee list, not the wall at large.
  • Consent, on the same screen. At 22:45 you'll see how many people here wrote about you tonight.
  • Screening unchanged. A night pass changes who may write. It changes nothing about what may be written.

The hour, the list, and two migrations

A pairing function that, for everybody who wrote that night, returns their count and any pair where each wrote about the other, fired once over the Realtime channel the wall already uses. A pair is disclosed only to the two people in it, simultaneously — the double-blind, applied to letters. A person who did not write receives nothing: not a count, not a zero, not a “no one this time.”

Then the guest list, whose arrival column is what turns an anecdote into a sponsor deck and cannot be collected retroactively. And before anybody is invited: apply the opt-out migration, and add the per-name ceiling the attack ledger says is missing — five letters to one name in a day, the sixth held for the desk. The allowance today is on the writer, never the receiver, so twenty writers with one letter each is twenty letters to one person in a night.

The two clocks

Letters lapse in thirty days, and nothing in the manual accounts for it. On a monthly cadence that is the difference between a wall that compounds and a wall that empties: run it naively and every cycle starts thinner than the last, and the search-to-found rate — which is what drives word of mouth — falls every month.

So the night sits on day 21, when the wall is at its fullest, and the letters written at the door are the next cycle's seed. The room refills the wall it just emptied, which is the compounding loop and it is free. Never extend the thirty days to make a wall look busier: the lapse is doing privacy work, and a thin wall is information.

Autumn 2026 has one job: prove that a room fills off the back of letters, and that the hour fires. Two numbers — heads, and pairs.

Kill criteria, set now

Criteria decided after the result is known are not criteria.

  • Under 200 letters by day 18. The room has no material. Stop, do not book, do not spend the night budget.
  • Under 120 heads on night one. The room does not fill and the app is a hobby.
  • Under 25% of reached people open their letter. The wall is not landing and nothing downstream matters.
  • Fewer than five pairs at the hour, with three letters each confirmed. The ceremony does not exist. This kills the room, not the product — the wall survives it.
  • Ten venue conversations with no all-ages room under $600. There is no room business at Berkeley.

Who owns what

The room needs a body in America on a Tuesday, and the founder is in Korea until April 2027. That is not a scheduling inconvenience; it is a structural fact about which layers can be personally owned.
