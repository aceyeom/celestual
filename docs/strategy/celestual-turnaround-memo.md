# Celestual: The Turnaround Memo

Oct 13, 2026 · @Ace

> Charts and diagrams are in the PDF version, [celestual-turnaround-memo.pdf](./celestual-turnaround-memo.pdf), and in the live doc.

## The verdict

Celestual as it stands doesn't work, and no feature or ambassador will fix it.

- About 300,000 views produced 145 sign-ups and 90 letters, half of them from four accounts.
- 54 people sealed a note. Only 16 of those notes were aimed at someone also on Celestual, and none became a mutual that wasn't set up in advance.
- Two-thirds of the hearts on the wall (129 of 197) were added by code, and the business has made one $2.99 sale.

**The cause is arithmetic, not marketing.** A mutual needs both people on the app at the same time, and strangers from a viral video almost never are. So Celestual stops being an app people keep and becomes one night a group runs for itself: **"\[group\]'s 11:11"**. Everyone in the room knows each other. Signed thank-yous always arrive, and sealed picks open privately at 11:11 pm only when both people chose the same one.

### What to do, in this order

Until discharge (about April 2027) this is a $0 test, not a launch.

1. **Get a private lawyer's answer** on whether you may run the company at all while serving. Don't ask through your unit unless the lawyer says to.
2. **Pay the trust debts this week.** Remove the invented hearts, hide the team's 45 letters, and lock the public table that logs who looked up whom. One screenshot of any of them ends any campus plan.
3. **Find a host (a club officer who will run a night for their group) who commits in writing,** before more than 10 hours of code. Start with Korean student associations and co-ed clubs, aiming at Pepero Day (11.11).
4. **Only then build the 11:11, version 0:** names only, three kinds of pick, a private reveal, about 28 to 40 of your own hours with Claude writing the code.
5. **Judge every result against pass-or-stop tests ("gates") written now** and held by someone outside the team:
   - whether nights run from Korea through hosts work (Gate 1), ten days after the third night
   - romance at Gate 2, on 1 March 2027
   - "is the format real?" in June 2027
   - "is it a company?" in December 2027, after a term in person

**Expect November to be small.** There are about even odds it shrinks to one pilot on 20 November.

A good night of about 45 people gives roughly 100 thank-yous, 5 to 15 "closer friends" pairs and 0 to 2 "i like you" pairs. One night says nothing about romance. That needs about 650 "i like you" picks, likely from more than 1,100 people. So 1 March will probably read "not proven yet", and that is not a failure.

### Your questions, in a line each

- **Why did 300,000 views become about 45 letters?** The videos sold a feeling with no action attached, asked viewers to try something they knew would fail, took seven steps to reach a ping, and couldn't be measured.
- **Does Celestual stay an app?** No. It becomes a night a group opens: a page reached by one DM, used for one week around one 11:11. Never a native app.
- **How do the features change?** Kill what fakes engagement, freeze the wall and the always-on note, and build only the group 11:11. The table is under "Product".
- **Is the mutual ping capped by density?** Yes, permanently, at global scale: 55 sealed notes from real users, only 16 aimed at someone on Celestual, and no mutual that friends didn't set up in advance. You can't lift the cap, so you shrink the world to one group and one night.
- **Is the wall just the Unsent Project?** Yes, a far smaller one: about 45 organic letters against their millions. Clean it, keep it as a quiet front porch, and stop building on it.
- **Should you hire sorority or community-college ambassadors?** No, paid or unpaid.
  - Celestual already ran a recruiting program and retired it with four rows of data.
  - Community colleges lack the dense groups this needs.
  - Sororities can host in spring through their councils, never as paid promoters.

### Ambition, honestly

No plan lifts the cap in six months, because the cap is that two people must be in one place at one time. So the ambition goes into a high bar for December 2027 (40 or more groups running nights, half of them with no founder help) and the in-person launch in 2027, not into spending now.

If the gates pass, the big version is real: groups that rebook every term, campus-wide 11:11s, Korea's own calendar of confession days (Pepero Day, White Day), and a fall 2027 push with the founders on the ground. Even then, the odds of a durable company are roughly 1 in 10 to 1 in 20.

The 300,000 people who watched a film about an ex are not this plan's customer, and that's fine.

## Diagnosis: the marketing worked, the product can't convert it

The content did its job: it found people with something unsaid. Growth is capped because those people land in two products that can't pay off for them, and nothing they do brings in the next person.

**What the production database says (13 October 2026):**

- **145 people** have signed in since 30 July; 98 proved an Instagram handle, 6 a school address.
- **90 letters, 79 live.** Four accounts wrote 45 of them (17, 14, 9 and 5), in bursts that read like the team seeding the wall. Everyone else, 39 people, wrote 45 (40 live), mostly in the two weeks after the late-September content.
- **72 pings from 56 people.** Only 16 of the 55 standing notes from real users (29%) point at someone who is on Celestual at all.
- **11 mutuals ever, between 19 people.** In 9 of them one side had joined within a day of the match, and 4 involve one of those four accounts. They were set up between friends. The organic mutual rate is about zero. The reveals on 3 and 10 October produced none.
- **One purchase, $2.99.** 17 people were active in the last seven days.
- **Nothing measures a view becoming a visit.** Web analytics is not enabled, and every letter's source reads `direct`.

The loss happens at both ends. Almost nobody who watched came. The few who came mostly wrote to people who will never be here, so nothing could ever open.

Both spikes followed a push (the Berkeley launch week, then the late-September content) and were gone within two weeks. Between pushes the product gains about five people a week and almost no letters.

### Six reasons growth is capped

1. **Two products with opposite physics.** The wall is single-player and works at zero users: you write to someone and feel lighter. The ping is two-player and works only when both people are present at the same moment.
   - The wall's writers are mostly writing to exes, people who moved away, and in one case someone who died. Those are the people least likely to ever be on Celestual and send one back.
   - So the wall's demand is piped into the one product it can never pay off. Only 9 of the 43 people who ever wrote a letter placed a ping.
2. **The ping is capped by density, and the weekly reveal cut it further.** A mutual needs three things to be true at once: you're both on Celestual in the same window, they feel it too, and they name you (formula below). With 145 people spread across several countries, the first of those is close to zero for almost everyone.
   - Since 27 September a note runs only to the next Saturday, and keeping it alive costs a ping. Two people who both care must now act within the same week or so, instead of within 60 days.
   - Shrinking a note's life from 60 days to about a week cuts the chance that both notes are live at once by roughly eight times. The match record can't show it yet, because nearly every mutual so far was arranged between friends.
3. **The wall is the Unsent Project without the archive.** The Unsent Project's draw is volume: so many letters that you can search your own name and find one. Celestual has 79, and half of those came from four accounts. At catharsis, the job both products do, Celestual is strictly worse at this size.
   - Its one real difference, that a letter here could actually be answered, is not used anywhere in the product.
4. **The content delivered the feeling instead of the action.** A film or skit about unsent love gives the viewer the catharsis inside the video, so watching is the whole experience.
   - The ask that followed was a test most viewers know will fail. Asked "do they still think about you?", most think of someone who isn't on this app.
   - The path was long: video, profile, link, the wall in Instagram's browser, write, then a DM code to ping, then wait for Saturday. There was no deadline and no reason to act today.
   - The result: about 0.05% of views became a person, and about 0.015% an organic letter.
5. **Nothing brings the next person.** Nothing a writer or sender does is designed to pull in someone else, and the open-door Story card from the growth framework was never built. Each spike in the chart above decays to near zero in about ten days, so every new person is bought with founder content effort.
6. **Craft has outrun evidence.** 77 migrations, a council, nine gates, a logo lab and a 35-second film, for 145 people and 17 weekly actives. In two weeks the posture changed on campus scope, open letters, replies, sharing, money and the reveal schedule, with no measurement to judge any of it.

The chance that one note becomes a mutual:

```latex
P(\text{mutual}) = P(\text{they are here in the same window}) \times P(\text{they feel it too}) \times P(\text{they name you})
```

The product can't change the second term. All of growth strategy is the first term, and the plan below attacks it with time rather than headcount.

**What did work, and must be kept:** a strong emotional hook (300k views), people writing deeply and sincerely in five languages, and a privacy design that makes the honest version of this product possible.

## The user: who actually shows up, and what they want

The people Celestual attracts mostly want to say something to someone who is gone. The people who can make a mutual happen want to know about someone who is still here. The product serves the first group's feeling and needs the second group's behaviour.

**Who writes (shares of the 40 live letters from the 39 people outside those four accounts):**

| Segment | Share of organic letters | What they write | Their person | Can their note become a mutual? | Role in the plan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Mourner | 58% (23) | an ex, an apology, "come back", "do you still think of me" | gone: another city, another relationship, sometimes dead | almost never: the person is not in their world any more | audience, content supply, the reason people watch |
| The Hopeful | 15% (6) | a crush, a situationship, "do I have a chance?" | here: same school, club or friend group | yes, and they are the only segment that produces mutuals | the engine; small today, must become the target |
| The Appreciator | 10% (4) | gratitude, a friend's inside joke, "you light up the room" | here, and they want them to see it | partly: they want the letter found, not sealed | the vector: letters people want the subject to find |
| Noise | 12% (5) | jokes, tests, a single emoji | none | no | moderation load |
| Grief | 5% (2) | someone who died | gone | no | handle with care; never marketing material |

Those shares come from reading each of those letters; the categories are judgment calls. Two more groups never write and outnumber everyone above. **Readers** are the 24 people who left hearts, and the much larger number who read and left. **Searchers** came to look for their own name and left when it wasn't there. Nobody measures either.

### The psychology that matters

- **The real question is "do they think about me?", not "how do I confess?"** It is the question the founders' own film ends on. The product forbids answering it unless the other person independently asks too, which for a Mourner means never.
- **Fear of rejection beats desire, until a deadline arrives.** Removing all risk also removes all urgency: if nothing bad happens by waiting, people wait. Every proven format in this space has a deadline: graduation, Valentine's Day, the end of a survey window.
- **The feeling is episodic and triggered by moments.** Anniversaries, holidays, seeing their Story, the end of a semester, New Year's Eve. Celestual has no moments, only a weekly clock that runs on Pacific time.
- **Sharing has to make the sharer look desired, never needy.** Nobody posts "I wrote to someone". People will post "I'm open to hearing it", and they will send a friend "is this about you?"

### Where they are

- **They arrive from Instagram,** inside its in-app browser, which is also where magic-link sign-in breaks (the mail opens in another browser).
- **They are global and diaspora-heavy.** 71 of 90 letters carry no campus. The letters mention Berkeley, USC, New York and Busan, and are written in English, Korean, Mandarin pinyin, Turkish, Spanish and French.
- **The Berkeley push didn't take.** 19 letters carry Berkeley, but only 3 came from outside the four most active accounts. It also produced 6 school-address proofs and one logged card scan.
- **The founders are in Korea.** Any plan that needs them standing on a US campus before 2027 fails on contact.

**The implication:** the audience is global and the mechanic is local. The plan has to manufacture local density inside a global audience, by gathering people into the same moment and the same small groups.

## Framing: does Celestual stay an app?

No. Celestual stops being an always-on app and becomes a ritual a group runs for itself, delivered as a link. Everything that works in this category sits in the same corner of the map.

## Product: keep the engine, stop faking, build one thing

Keep the privacy engine and the proof of identity. Kill everything that invents engagement or waits for global density. Build exactly one new thing: a group's 11:11 night.

### Your two questions, answered

**Is the mutual ping capped by density? Yes, permanently, at global scale.** Of 55 sealed notes from real users, 16 point at someone who is on Celestual, and none has ever opened without being arranged between friends.

- No amount of content fixes this. A New Year's night for 3,000 strangers would open somewhere between 2 and 17 two-way notes in total.
- Inside a group where everyone knows everyone, the same mechanic gives a 45-person night about 100 thank-yous, 5 to 15 two-way "closer friends" pairs and 0 to 2 romantic ones.
- Even then, a small group produces some mutuals by pure chance. So the plan judges romance only against a chance baseline, and only after about 650 "i like you" picks.

**Is the wall just the Unsent Project? Yes, a smaller and partly invented one.** It has 79 letters against their 5 million, half written by four accounts. About two-thirds of the hearts it shows were never pressed: 129 invented against 68 real (migration 0059 adds them on purpose).

Its honest job from now on is a quiet front porch and a mood board for the brand, kept clean and never shown to a host as "the product".

### The one thing to build: 11:11, version 0

One page per group night, at an unguessable address, kept fully separate from the existing ping engine and its Instagram automations.

- **Join.** Each night has its own secret keyword or link, posted only in the group's chat, never on a card, sticker or public post. DM it to @celestual.us and get a single-use link tied to your Instagram account. Confirm a birth month and year (18 or older), type a first name and last initial, and give an email for the 11:11 pm result, with that purpose stated on the screen.
  - Anyone who joins without the night's own link waits in a one-tap approval queue for the host or the operator (the host's second officer, who helps run the night).
  - A join never triggers the old automations: no mutual news, no tags, just a plain reply.
- **Pick.** Names only, no text, in three kinds:
  1. *thanks / 고마워요*: signed, up to 10, always delivered
  2. *closer friends / 친해지고 싶어요*: sealed, up to 3
  3. *i like you / 좋아해요*: sealed, up to 2
- **Open.** A sealed pick opens only if both people chose the same kind for each other, and the pick screen says so: "it opens only if they picked the same one. pick the one you mean." Picks can be changed or withdrawn until 10:11 pm. Results appear at 11:11 pm local, privately, on the page and by a neutral email, never by DM.
- **Everyone opens something.** Officers split the joined list so every member receives at least one real, signed thank-you from an officer before 10:11 pm; officers may send up to 40 on their own night. The page never shows a bare zero.
- **Who can see what.** Members see the directory, never picks. A member can silently mute someone, which also blocks that person's thank-yous; removing someone is left to the operator. The host and operator see counts only. The pick screen says plainly that the two founders could technically read picks until deletion, and every read is logged.
- **After.** At 71 hours a job saves the aggregate numbers the gates need; at 72 hours picks that went one way are deleted. Two-way pairs keep their names for 14 days (for "did you talk?"), then become anonymous, and are deleted at Gate 2. The result page asks: tell me about the next one; do it again?; did you know?
- **Run.** The founders create each night. The operator gets a key that works for that one night only and does two things: pause the reveal, or remove someone. Nobody in a participating group ever gets the Instagram inbox or the desk. Reports go through a form to a named responder outside the group who is awake during the reveal; no responder, no night. Host terms bar any group event or alcohol at reveal time and say honestly when reports are read. Rosters are reported as a count, never a screenshot of names.
- **Size.** About 50 to 70 engineering hours, or 28 to 40 founder hours with Claude writing the code, including the join. None of it starts until a host has committed in writing; before that, only a clickable demo.

Version 1 comes only after Gate 1 passes. It adds one line each after a mutual (moderated), a letter to the whole group, "sorry" and "formal?" kinds, and picks that can stand until the group's next night.

## Design, brand and content: keep the look, change the promise

Keep the name and the pixel-phone look. Change what they promise: not "find out if they still think about you", which almost never comes true, but one specific night that does: "\[group\]'s 11:11".

**The positioning, in one breath:** for people in a close group who have something they've never said to someone in it, a Celestual 11:11 is a one-night ritual. Everyone sends sealed notes to people in the group, and at 11:11 that night the ones that went both ways open, for those two only. Unlike a confession page, nothing is ever posted. Unlike a crush app, it's for anything unsaid, and nobody learns they were chosen unless they chose back.

### The name

Keep Celestual for now; renaming costs months and there is no evidence yet that the name is the problem. Set a trigger instead. If 3 or more of the first 10 hosts misspell it or ask whether it's an astrology app, rename before April. Until then the host's name and "11:11" do the talking, and every link is tapped or scanned, never typed.

### Design rules for 11:11

- **Reuse the pixel phone.** The group's short name sits in the carrier slot where "CAL" sits now, the clock reads 11:11, and there is one lit key: "count me in". At most 45 words above the fold.
- **A hard budget.** No new colours, glyphs or type sizes, and at most three new screens: the landing, the directory with picks, and the result.
- **A plain front door for hosts.** Host and risk-chair material is a one-page document, not the phone. Hosts land on a plain `/host` page, never on the wall. The 11:11 page never links to the wall, the paywall or any face.
- **Design the empty result first.** Most people will open no two-way pick. They see the thank-yous they received (officers make sure there is at least one), then "nothing opened both ways this time. that's how most people's night goes." Never a bare zero.

### Content: what to stop, and its one job now

**Stop** selling exes and grief ("do they still think about you?"), which recruits the people least likely to ever match. Also stop cinematic films, brand-only posts, "find out if it's mutual", "search your name", anything seeded or invented (films included), and filming anyone's reveal.

**Content's only job until Gate 1 is host leads,** at no more than 3 founder hours a week:

- archive the old grid of letters to exes, and set the bio to "a private 11:11 for your group. nothing is ever posted."
- three pinned posts: what an 11:11 is, what nobody ever sees, and run one for your group
- one post a week at most: the format, or a gratitude letter whose writer opted in, ending "comment HOST"; a bilingual Pepero Day explainer for Korean student associations
- after each night, an honest recap with true counts above the floors and a quote from the host

Measure it as at least one HOST lead per 10,000 views, and at least 5 qualified host calls from content by 3 November. The film and the aesthetic aren't wasted. They become the senior "last 11:11" campaign in May, once there are real nights to show.

## Growth: hosts, not ads

Stop buying attention for a product that can't convert it. From now on, growth comes one host at a time, and each host brings a whole group's members into the same night. That is the only kind of growth that raises the odds that both people are on Celestual at the same time.

### Why the UGC, films and skits didn't convert

They worked as entertainment and failed as a funnel, for four reasons:

1. **The video was the payoff.** It delivered the catharsis itself.
2. **The ask was a test most viewers knew would fail.** Their person isn't on the app.
3. **There was no deadline and no group.** Nobody had a reason to act today, together.
4. **There was no measurement.** Nobody could tell which post worked.

More of the same, or better versions of it, buys another spike that is gone in ten days.

### Your ambassador idea: sororities and community colleges

**Not now, and not as paid ambassadors.** Celestual has already tried this twice and its own migrations record the result:

- The recruitment competition (migration 0016) was retired in 0034 with four rows of data in total.
- The campus windows (0006) were retired in 0035 with their tables empty, because they never had a way in.

Paying people to promote a product with no loop and no night produces exactly the "mid launch" you're afraid of.

| Group | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sororities | **Spring 2027, through councils, never paid per head** | They are the right shape: dense, Instagram-native, with formal season where "would you go with me?" is a real fear. They are the wrong ask in November. A single-gender chapter can't make mutuals unless a paired fraternity joins too. Chapters run new-member periods into November, and risk chairs veto anything that sounds like an anonymous crush app. Paid reps read as MLM to their own sisters. |

| Korean student associations, co-ed K-pop, dance and cultural clubs | **Yes, first, in November** | Mixed-gender, dense, Instagram-native, with a guaranteed weekly meeting and no national office to veto. They already celebrate 11.11 as Pepero Day, and the founders' own network reaches them. |
| Campus crush or confession pages | **Never as hosts** | They are pile-on machines with no roster and no boundary. |

### The host engine

1. **Census first (by Fri 16 Oct).** Each founder lists named people they know in US Korean student associations and co-ed clubs: the org, its active roster, and the relationship. Until the lawyer answers, these are personal soundings only: no company materials, no written commitments.
2. **Pitch their ritual, not your app.** "We'll run a private 11:11 for your Pepero Day." Only if the lawyer clears paying and buying (Branch B, see Money below), add: "and we'll buy from your Pepero sale", a fixed quantity at their price, whatever the turnout. Someone not in service places the order, and their post carries a sponsorship line. The packs carry no join path, go to members at the group's own event and never to public tables, and the first call with the host checks the school's rules on outside sponsorship.
3. **Cold outreach waits for the right moment.** 300 officer emails from campus organisation directories go out 1 to 15 December and 15 January to 5 February, aimed at incoming boards for the Valentine's wave. In October they would only reach boards about to turn over, and eat the build week.
4. **Two contacts per host, and a standby host for every date.** Hosts ghost; that is the most common failure.
5. **The in-room moment is the whole night.** In the first five minutes of the group's meeting, everyone joins with the night's own keyword, posted only in the group chat, and sends one signed thank-you. Sealed picks are made later, alone. The review panel's estimate: a push in the group chat alone gets 10 to 15% of members; five minutes in the room gets 40 to 60%.
6. **End every night by booking the next one.** The host announces the next date in the group chat, and every result page asks whether you're in another group that should run one. Rebooking and spreading are the two numbers that decide whether this is a company.

**The group card does the posting, not individuals.** "\[group\]'s 11:11 · send 고마워요 to anyone in \[group\]; it opens at 11:11 pm. you can also pick 'closer friends' or 'i like you'. those open only if they picked you the same way. if not, they're never told. the link is in the group chat." The host posts it from their own account, which is herd cover by design: being in says nothing about who you picked. Use "11.11 at 11:11" for the event, not the PEPERO trademark. No individual "I'm in" cards in version 0.

## Money: earn nothing, spend almost nothing, ask the lawyer first

Celestual should take no money until a Korean lawyer says in writing what the founders may do while in service. After that, revenue waits for proof that groups come back.

### Why the $2.99 pings can't work

- **There is nothing to buy yet.** One purchase in two weeks, and zero organic mutuals for a ping to pay for.
- **Each buyer pays about once.** Since 29 September a bought ping that doesn't open comes back as credit, so a buyer rarely needs to buy again.
- **It sells the wrong thing.** Extra guesses for curious people is the "pay to find out" shape that NGL's paid hints were punished for.
- **It may not be legal for the founders.** Korea's Framework Act on Military Status and Service (Article 30) restricts profit-making activity by serving soldiers. That is a personal risk to the founders, and a sale has already happened. This document is not legal advice, which is why the first step is a lawyer.

### The legal question: a private lawyer first, written answer by Wed 21 Oct

Ask a lawyer privately: the free Korea Legal Aid Corporation (대한법률구조공단), or a paid consult of about $50 to $150, budgeted outside the $0 rule. Contact your unit or the Military Manpower Administration (병무청) only if the lawyer tells you to. An inquiry through the chain of command puts possible profit-making on record before you have counsel.

3. pay or donate to a student organisation

### What Celestual could become

Looked at from far enough away, Celestual's real asset is not the wall. It is a trustworthy way for two people to find out they both want the same thing, without either one going first.

1. **Nights for groups (now to May 2027).** One night, one group, sealed notes that open only if they go both ways.
2. **Nights for every awkward ask (2027).** The same mechanic for formal and date-party invitations, closer friendships, "I'm sorry, I'd talk if you would", roommates and project partners. These are categories where reciprocity is much higher than for crushes, so more people get a yes.
3. **The mutual layer for communities (2028 and later).** Clubs, alumni groups and associations run their own nights without founder contact, and pay or are sponsored to.
4. **The letters, as culture, alongside all of it.** A small media brand of the unsaid, from consented public letters only: books, exhibits, a yearly report with strict floors.

What it should never become: a "someone likes you, pay to find out who" app (the Gas and NGL shape), a dating app, or a feed.

**Keeping the count honest.**

- **"Chance" means something precise.** It is the number of pairs you'd get by shuffling everyone's picks at random within their own night, keeping how many each person sent and received. It is computed over 10,000 shuffles, then doubled, because there is no gender field and most romantic picks cross genders. Without the doubling, chance alone would pass the gate about 9 times in 10. So only co-ed nights count toward the romance verdict; single-gender nights, and nights where the host switched "i like you" off, are reported separately.
- **Mutuals that don't count.** Never counted: mutuals among the founders, the producer, host officers, the four most active accounts, the 19 people in past mutuals, and anyone who joined through a test link. Also excluded: pairs where one side joined through the other's personal link, and pairs who tap "we planned this" or "already together" (no answer counts as not clean).
- **Timing flags.** Pairs who picked within three minutes of each other on the same network are flagged and reported both ways.
- **The analysis is tested before it is trusted.** The gatekeeper reviews the 71-hour snapshot before the sheet is frozen. By 31 January the analysis is run on two fakes: a 500-person room with no real feelings must fail, and the 11 old arranged mutuals must be caught.
