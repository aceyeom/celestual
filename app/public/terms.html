02 What the service does

  • You place a ping on an Instagram handle. That person is never told, and nothing is ever posted, messaged, followed or liked on your behalf.
  • It resolves only if that person independently places one on you. You both find out at the same moment, or neither of you ever does. A one sided ping is shown to nobody.
  • You may hold two standing pings at once. Each stands for sixty days and then lapses. Renewing is free and unlimited. Letting one go frees its slot the same day.
  • You may put up to twenty words with a ping. They stay sealed unless it turns out to be mutual.
  • After you place one you are shown a single fact about that handle: whether it has reached celestual yet. It says nothing about who has pinged whom, and nothing about anybody's feelings or activity.
  • To be revealed to anybody you first have to prove your handle, by sending a one time code to our Instagram account from that account. A mutual is never revealed to an unproved handle.
  • When it is mutual we may tell both people by email, if there is an address on file, and by Instagram direct message inside the window Meta allows. That is the only message celestual ever sends anybody about another person.
  • Placing a ping is rate limited, and capped over a rolling window, so the service cannot be used to sweep a list of names.

The numbers above are the ones in force today. We can change how the mechanic works as the product changes.

03 The wall

At a participating campus, celestual carries a wall: short anonymous letters, each addressed to one Instagram handle. These are its rules.

  • The index is public. The handles that carry letters, and how many each carries, are open to anybody with no address and no account. That is how a person finds their own name.
  • The first five letters are open to anybody. After that a letter arrives with nothing readable in it, and it opens on either proof: a verified email address at that campus, or a verified Instagram handle.
  • Writing takes the campus address, and three letters in any seven days.
  • A letter stands for thirty days and then comes off by itself.
  • Every letter is screened before it appears. Software reads it first, a person reads anything the software is unsure of, and a letter that fails the screen is stored and never published.
  • Anybody who has passed either proof can report a letter. Reporting takes it off immediately, before anybody reviews it.
  • The person a letter is about can take their whole name off the wall from a verified handle. Every letter written to that handle goes with it, no desk can put it back, and there is no appeal for them to answer. Taking the handle off celestual entirely does the same thing, on every campus and for good.
  • We do not disclose who wrote a letter to anybody, including the person it is about, unless the author is asked and agrees. A valid court order is the one thing that could compel us, and there is very little we could produce.
