01 Who can use it

You must be 18 or older. If you are under 18, do not use celestual. When we learn that somebody on it is under 18 we take the account off.

Use it as yourself. Prove a handle only if you hold that account, use only an email address you control, and do not let anybody else send a note, write a letter or a reply, or buy pings through your access.

You can sign in with a link we mail to an email address, a school address included, with a Google account, or by confirming your Instagram handle with one direct message to our Instagram account. A mailed link works once, for thirty minutes, and signs in the browser that opens it. Whoever can read an inbox can sign in with its address, so use one that only you can read.

We run celestual from the United States and offer it there. If you open it from somewhere else, that is your own decision and your own local law applies to you.

02 What the service does

  • You send a private note to an Instagram handle. That person is never told, and nothing is ever posted, messaged, followed or liked on your behalf.
  • Every note runs to a reveal, on Saturday at 9pm California time: the first one at least a day after the note is sent. If the person it is to has sent one to you, the pair is sealed from the moment the second note is sent until the next reveal, and you both find out that night, at the same moment. If not, neither of you ever does. A note that is not mutual is shown to nobody.
  • A note that is not mutual lapses at its reveal. It stays listed to you for seven days, so that you can send it again, and then it is deleted. You can keep a running note for the following week, one week ahead at a time.
  • Letting a note go before its reveal deletes it the same day and tells nobody anything. A note that has turned out mutual cannot be let go.
  • Sending a note spends a ping. You have one free ping for every Saturday reveal, and you can buy more. Nobody can have more than ten pings in one reveal. Section 08 has the rules.
  • A note can carry up to 280 characters of your own words. They stay sealed unless it turns out to be mutual. A note whose words carry a link, an email address, a phone number, a street address or a slur is not sent.
  • After you send one you are shown a single fact about that handle: whether it has reached celestual yet. It says nothing about who has sent a note to whom, and nothing about anybody's feelings or activity.
  • To send a note you first prove your own handle, by sending a one time code to our Instagram account from that account. A mutual is never revealed to an unproved handle.
  • When it is mutual we tell both people on the night of the reveal, by email if there is an address on file, and by Instagram direct message inside the window Meta allows. That is the only message celestual ever sends anybody that names another person.
  • Sending notes is rate limited, so the service cannot be used to sweep a list of names.

The numbers above are the ones in force today. We can change how the mechanic works as the product changes.

03 The wall

celestual carries a wall: short anonymous letters, each written to one Instagram handle or to a first name. These are its rules.

  • The wall is public. Every letter on it, and the names the letters are written to, can be read by anybody, with no account.
  • A letter never prints the handle it is written to. It opens with the writer's greeting, or with the first name on that handle's public profile, and it is filed under the handle so that the person it is to can find it.
  • Anybody can write a letter. It is screened before it goes up: software reads it first, a person reads anything the software is unsure of, and a letter that fails the screen is stored and never shown.
  • A writer who has confirmed a berkeley.edu address can post as a Berkeley student instead. That letter carries the Berkeley mark and goes up at once. It is screened where it stands, and it comes down if it fails.
  • We limit how many letters can be written, and we can change the limit.
  • A letter stands for thirty days and then comes off by itself.
  • Anybody can heart a letter. A heart shows as a count, and nobody is shown who gave it.
  • Anybody signed in, by any of the ways in section 01, can report a letter. Reporting takes it off at once, before anybody reviews it, and a person at the desk decides whether it goes back.
  • The person a letter is written to, once they have proved that handle is theirs, can remove it from the wall, and can undo that within a day. They can also ask to be emailed when somebody writes them a letter. That mail says only that a letter was written, never who wrote it, and it carries a link that removes the letter.
  • They can also take their whole name off the wall. Every letter written to that handle goes with it, the name cannot be written to again, no desk can put it back, and there is no appeal for them to answer. Taking the handle off celestual entirely does the same thing, for good.
  • We do not tell anybody who wrote a letter, including the person it is about. A valid legal demand is the one thing that could compel us to produce what we hold about the account or device it came from, and the privacy policy lists what that is.

Replies

A letter can carry replies under it. Anybody can read them and like them. These are the rules for writing one, and you accept them before your first.

  • Replying takes a confirmed school email address. The one exception is the person the letter is written to, once they have proved that Instagram handle is theirs: they can reply without one, and their replies are marked as theirs.
  • Replies are anonymous to other people. Each writer is shown as a small creature with a one word name, the same all the way down one thread and different under the next letter, so nobody can follow a writer from one thread to another.
  • Replies are not anonymous to us. We keep who wrote each reply, so that we can act on abuse, and we do not show it to anybody else, including the person the letter is about.
  • A reply never names or tags anybody else. No @, no handle and no full name of anybody, whether or not they are on celestual.
  • Nothing hateful, threatening or sexual about a person, nothing that exposes something private about them, and no contact details, addresses, timetables or anything that says where somebody will be.
  • Every reply is screened before it appears. Software reads it first, and a reply the software is unsure of waits for a person and is shown to nobody but you until it passes. A reply that fails the screen is refused and kept in our records.
  • Anybody can report a reply. A reply reported from three different devices comes off until a person reviews it.
  • The person a letter is written to can stop new replies, leaving the ones already there, or put the whole thread away so that nobody else sees it, and open it again.
  • We limit how many replies can be written, in a day and under any one letter.
  • Abuse has consequences. We can take a reply down, take away your ability to reply or to use the wall, and where the law or your school's own rules require it, report what was written to your school.
