Privacy

last updated 8 september 2026

celestual is built to hold as little as possible about who you are interested in. The handle you pinged is stored as a salted one way hash and never as a name, so a database dump, an employee and a subpoena all come back with a string nobody can read.

This page is Celestual LLC's privacy policy, and it covers celestual.us and the campus wall. It is the whole of what we hold.

01 What we hold

your handle
In plain text, once you have proved it with the code you send our Instagram account. It is the identity the whole product runs on.
who you pinged
As a salted one way hash, never as a handle. Two pings are matched by comparing hashes. Nothing in that column can be turned back into a name, by us or by anybody who takes the database.
your twenty words
The line you put with a ping, if you wrote one. It is sealed unless the ping turns out to be mutual, and it is purged with the ping if it lapses.
an email address
If you leave one, so we can tell you about a mutual or a ping coming up on its sixty days. Optional everywhere except the campus wall, which needs an address at that campus.
a campus address
The address that opened the wall, and when it was proved. It is held apart from anything you write, and the composer never sees it.
what you wrote
A letter, the handle it is addressed to, and the record of the screening it passed. No author is stored against it.
a heart
That a letter was hearted, and by how many. Nothing anywhere returns who.
a session
The hash of a token your browser makes. The token itself never leaves your device, so reading that table does not let anybody sign in as you.
five reads
At most five rows saying that a browser opened a letter, so the first five on the wall can be free without the count being the reader's to keep. They carry no identity, cannot be joined to a session, and are deleted the moment that browser passes a gate.
operational records
Timestamps, rate limit counters, the IP address a request came from, and the record of a screening, a report or a takedown.
a bar
If a handle is taken off, the salted hash of it, so the refusal holds. It contains nothing else.

02 What we cannot produce

Some of this is a promise. Some of it is arithmetic, and the arithmetic comes first.

  • A one sided ping is revealed to nobody. Not to the person it points at, not to another user, not to us.
  • Nobody who breaches the database reads the map of who wanted whom, because it is not written down. The plain handle exists only on the sender's own device.
  • We cannot hand anybody the list of people you have pinged. Not you, not a parent, not a court. There is nothing to read. A mutual is the exception, and it is not a secret: once two people have found each other, both handles are on the record.

And the promises:

  • Nothing is posted, messaged, followed or liked on your behalf, and the person you pinged is not told. The one message celestual ever sends about another person is the one that says it is mutual, to both of you.
  • There is no browsing, no profile, no ranking, no feed, and no list of who is on the service.
  • The single fact shown after you place a ping is whether that handle has reached celestual. It is never a hint about anybody's feelings or activity, and we do not build one.

03 How long it lasts

a standing ping
Sixty days. Then it lapses and the row is deleted outright. Renewing restarts the sixty, free and as often as you like. Letting one go deletes it the same day.
a mutual
While both people hold it. It goes when either of them takes their handle off.
a letter
Thirty days on the wall. A letter that was reported or refused is held out of sight instead of published, so that the decision can be looked at again.
a code
The code mailed to a campus address lasts ten minutes, dies after six wrong guesses, and is stored only as a hash. The code you send our Instagram account lasts until you send it or it lapses, and all it does is tie one message to one browser.
a session
Until it lapses or you sign out.
rate limit records
About two hours, which is the window they exist to enforce.
a bar
Kept, as a hash. A permanent refusal we forget is not permanent.
backups
Deleted rows can sit in an encrypted backup for a short period before it is overwritten. Nothing is restored from one except to recover the service.

04 Why we hold it

Each of the things in section 1 is there for one of four reasons: to run the service you asked for, to keep it from being turned against somebody, to keep it secure and working, or because you handed it to us and can take it back. If you are in the United Kingdom or the European Union, those are contract, legitimate interests, legitimate interests, and consent, in that order. Consent is withdrawn by taking the address off or by writing to us.

05 Looking a handle up

When you type a handle we look up that account's public profile so you can check you have the right person: the display name, the badge, and the picture. The lookup runs through Apify, a third party service, and it happens whether or not that account has ever touched celestual.

We keep the name, the badge and a copy of the picture on our own storage, so a screen keeps working and the same account is not looked up twice. It is public profile information and nothing else. Looking a handle up proves nothing about who is holding a browser and it never signs anybody in. Write to us if you want a cached profile dropped.

06 Who else handles it

Each of these handles data only to do a job for us, under a contract that says so.

  • Supabase, the database, the server functions and the file storage.
  • Vercel, which serves the site.
  • Resend, which sends the mail.
  • Apify, for the public profile lookups above.
  • Anthropic, which screens wall letters before they are published. A letter is sent to be read by a model and is not used to train one.
  • Meta and ManyChat, for the Instagram direct messages that prove a handle and carry a mutual. Your use of Instagram stays under Instagram's own terms.

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross context behavioural advertising, in the sense California gives those words. We have not done either in the past twelve months, and we do not plan to. There is no advertising on celestual and no third party analytics anywhere on it.

07 What we send you

  • That it is mutual. By email if there is an address on file, and by Instagram direct message inside the window Meta allows.
  • That a ping is coming up on its sixty days. Once per ping, five days out.
  • A code, when you are proving a campus address.
  • A link back in, when you ask for one.

There is no newsletter and no marketing mail. Every message above is about something you did.

08 Cookies, and your device

One cookie, and it is not a tracker: a random identifier the server sets so that a handle lookup can be rate limited without an account. It carries nothing about you.

Your browser also keeps things that never reach us: the plain text of the handles you have pinged, a draft letter, which letters you have opened, and the token that keeps you signed in. Clearing your browser storage clears all of it. There is no analytics cookie, no advertising cookie, and no third party tag on this site.

09 Your controls

the way out

Take a handle off permanently, whether or not it has ever been used here. One direct message from that account proves it is yours. No account to make, and nothing to ask us for.

celestual.us/optout

That is the deletion path as well. It bars the handle and erases everything that referenced it on either side. The deletion page says exactly what goes and what does not.

You can also write to contact@celestual.app and ask what we hold about a handle, ask us to correct it, ask for a copy in a portable file, or ask us to delete it. We answer within thirty days, and asking costs you nothing and changes nothing about how the service treats you. If we say no, you can appeal by replying to that answer. Because a handle is the identity here, we check a request the way the product checks everything else, with one direct message from the account.

Depending on where you live, those rights may come from California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia or Utah law, or from the United Kingdom and European Union rules, and you may complain to your own regulator. Since celestual sells nothing and carries no advertising, there is no sale or targeted advertising for a browser signal such as Global Privacy Control to switch off.

10 How it is kept

The browser cannot read any table in the database. Every path into the data is a small server function that returns a status and never somebody else's row. Targets are hashed, codes are hashed, sessions are hashed, and traffic is encrypted in transit. Production access belongs to the people who run the company and to nobody else. No system is perfect and we will not pretend this one is.

11 When the law asks

A valid legal demand can require us to hand over what we hold, and section 1 is that list. We will tell you when it happens unless we are forbidden to. What we cannot hand over is what was never written down: who you pinged, in a form anybody can read, does not exist here.

12 Where it is

celestual runs on servers in the United States. If you use it from somewhere else, what you send comes here and is handled under United States law, which may protect it differently from your own.

13 For adults

celestual is for people 18 and older. We do not knowingly collect anything from anybody under 18, and we delete it if we learn that we have. If you believe a child's information is here, write to us and we will act on it.

14 Changes, and contact

The date at the top moves when this page does, and that date is what it is for. Where a change is material we will say so on the site before it takes effect.
