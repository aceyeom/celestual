Privacy

last updated 3 september 2026

The shortest true version: we cannot read who your pings point at, and neither can anybody who ever breaches us.

1 · what we store

  • Your Instagram handle, in plain text, once you have proved it is yours.
  • Who you pinged, as a salted one way hash and never as a handle. A hash cannot be turned back into a name, by us or by anybody else, which is why the product can promise what it promises.
  • An email address, if you leave one. It is optional everywhere except the campus wall, which requires an address at that campus.
  • What you wrote, when you write a card or a letter. It is sealed until the moment it is opened, and a letter's author is never disclosed.
  • A session, as a hash of a token your browser holds. We store the hash; the token never leaves your device.
  • Ordinary operational records: timestamps, rate limit counters, and the IP address a request came from, kept only as long as the limit window they serve.

2 · what nobody ever learns

  • A one sided ping is revealed to nobody. Not to the person it points at, not to anybody else, not ever.
  • Nothing is posted, messaged, followed or notified on your behalf.
  • The only thing that surfaces is a pair who both entered each other, shown to exactly those two people at the same moment.
  • There is no browsing, no profiles, no list, and no feed.
  • The one fact you are shown after placing a ping is whether that handle has reached celestual yet. It says nothing about anybody's feelings or activity, and it is the only signal the product will ever give you about somebody else.

3 · the sixty days

A ping stands for sixty days. Renewing restarts them, free, as often as you feel it. Otherwise it lapses and the record is purged: unresolved longing self destructs instead of accumulating into an archive nobody wants to exist.

4 · public profile lookups

When you type a handle, we look up that account's public profile so you can confirm you have the right person: a display name, a verification badge, and a picture. That lookup goes through Apify, a third party scraping service, and it happens whether or not the account belongs to somebody using celestual.

We store the name, the badge and a copy of the picture on our own storage, so that a card keeps working and so the same account is never looked up twice. Looking a handle up proves nothing about who is using a browser, and it never signs anybody in.

5 · who else touches your data

  • Supabase, which hosts the database, the functions and the file storage.
  • Vercel, which serves the site.
  • Resend, which sends the mail.
  • Apify, for the public profile lookups above.
  • Anthropic, which screens wall letters before they are published. A letter is sent for screening and is not used to train anything.
  • Meta, for the Instagram direct message that proves a handle. Your use of Instagram stays subject to Instagram's own terms.

We do not sell anything to anybody, and there is no advertising on celestual.

6 · cookies

One, and it is not a tracker: a random identifier the server sets so a handle lookup can be rate limited without an account. It is not a fingerprint, it carries nothing about you, and there is no analytics or advertising cookie on this site.

7 · your data is yours

Take a handle off permanently, whether or not you use celestual. No account, no code, and nothing to ask us for.

celestual.us/optout

That is also the deletion path: it bars the handle from ever being entered again and erases everything that referenced it, on either side. The deletion instructions say what happens in more detail. For anything else, write to contact@celestual.app.

8 · for adults

Celestual is for people 18 and older. We do not knowingly collect anything from anybody under 18, and we delete it if we learn we have.

9 · changes

The date at the top moves when this does. Material changes are what that date is for.
