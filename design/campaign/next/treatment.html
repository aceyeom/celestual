every letter on the wall is to somebody.

One night in Berkeley, from the evening rush to sunrise, four people each write a letter on the wall. Each is to the next of them, and the last is to the first. None of them knows; only we see the round. It opens on a stranger giving up a seat on a bus, and it closes on the letter about it. Then, for the first time, all four worlds at once, each turning into its letter on the wall, and the last frame falls back into the first.

The frame is a split flap. Below the seam, the one writing. Above it, mirrored, the one they are writing to, in their own world and their own hour. When the letter goes up, its colour runs out of the phone through the writer's world, and when it reaches the seam the flap falls and brings the one above down, right way up, to write the next.

perspective
The wall's. Only we see the round; nobody in it is ever told they were written to.
the world
Four places in one city, each lit and brought down to four tones of a screen's colour. Grey until a letter goes up, and then its colour, cell by cell. The phone is the one crisp lit thing.
the sound
A round for four voices over a ground, one for each person, entering as each starts to write. The machine on its frames: every landing a click, the hinge catching, each module of the cascade, the clock's plates, a soft key for each word.
the end
The four at once on a contact sheet, each writer's phone still lit, each flipping into its letter on the wall, the line, the lockup, and a flap back into frame 0.

the round

A round in E flat over a four bar ground, Eb | Bb/D | Cm | Ab, played from real recordings. Each person is a voice entering at their own flap: sol a clarinet, wren a harp, pia a cello, yuna a horn at sunrise; under them a plucked double bass, and a strummed waltz until the walk takes over. Each voice enters with the tune and goes on through the answer, the walk and the long notes, so in bars 15 to 18 all four sound at once. Bar 19 holds the dominant under the trembling hinge, bar 20 comes home as the sheet lands, and in bar 21 each flip of the cascade sounds its writer's note, so the last chord is built a note at a time. node page/score.mjs --check holds it to chord tones on every downbeat and no parallel fifths or octaves.

what could go wrong

The chain has to read in one viewing: the greeting names the one above, the flap brings them down, the spine holds each frame together, the clock turns. Four letters in thirty seconds leaves each about four seconds on the phone and again on the wall. The dither has to survive the platforms: 6 px cells, lossless frames, and a private test upload before the film goes out.

script A · on file

two inks

30 s · 1080 × 1920 · 24 fps, the prints on twos · 120 bpm · F major · fifteen bars

a private note is one ink. a picture needs both.

Two people, each printed in their own ink: iris in rose, jun in ice. Alone, each print is half a picture: a woman on a bench at night, leaning her head on a shoulder that is not there; a man on the same bench resting his cheek on hair that is not there. Each sends theirs privately, and the press takes both inks and prints nothing. On Saturday at nine the press runs. The sheets that only ever had one ink come out blank, and nobody ever knows. The one that had both comes out whole, and where they touch, a colour neither ink had.

Somebody who sees it once can explain celestual to a friend: it only prints if you both send one.

perspective
The press's. It sees both halves, which neither of them can, and prints only pairs: the double blind, drawn as a machine.
the world
Every frame a riso print on warm paper, in rose, ice and black. Twelve prints a second, each a hair out of register. The words are printed too.
the sound
A hocket: one tune split between two players, each half full of holes until both play. From nine, the press is the rhythm section.
the end
The registration mark in the sheet's corner slides into register and becomes the mark.

the print rules

  • Paper #F4F0E4 and three inks: rose #DF93AF for her, ice #8FB8DC for him, black for the world and the words. Rose over ice, about #8371A9, only where they touch.
  • Inks are filters: nothing is lighter than paper, so a light is paper left bare in a field of ink (the moon, the scanner's bar).
  • Above 78 percent the dots join and print solid, with the drum's mottle. A dropout now and then.
  • The camera is a copy stand: straight down, sheets slid in and out on the beat. A cut is a new sheet; nothing dissolves.
  • Every sheet is cut to 9 by 16, the shape of a phone's glass.
  • Never a halftone filter over a render, a grunge pack, CMYK, or anything "vintage".

which one

make round.

It is the story the owner asked for, in the world the owner chose, and it shows the product as it is.

The composer, the greeting, the colour, the battery, being read and the wall itself, and nothing it does not do. It has a shape people keep (a kindness at the start, the letter about it at the end) and a form that pays for watching twice: it loops, and the second time the first scene means something. Its 15 second cut is a whole story and its 6 second cut is a joke that stands on its own.

Two inks stays on file. It is the cheaper film and a good one, but it is about the private note and its reveal, which was the first film's subject.
