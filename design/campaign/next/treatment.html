celestual · the next film · two scripts · october 2026

two inks, or pacific.

Two complete scripts for the second film, to choose between and refine. The first film was one person's night, looking back. Both of these are built on two people from the first frame, and on the moment the first film passed over on a split flap board: Saturday at nine. Each world is drawn live below, from the product's own parts.

  • looking back, to waitingThe framework keeps one finding for content: anticipation spreads and melancholy does not. Both films count down to one moment.
  • one person, to twoThe product only ever happens to two people. Neither film has a hero.
  • the black room, to a world eachInk on paper in daylight. The whole sky, night and day in one frame.
  • a song, to the mechanism in soundA tune split between two players. A figure on two sides of an ocean, locking into one at nine.
script A

two inks

30 s · 1080 × 1920 · 24 fps, the prints on twos · 120 bpm · F major · fifteen bars

a private note is one ink. a picture needs both.

Two people, each printed in their own ink: iris in rose, jun in ice. Alone, each print is half a picture: a woman on a bench at night, leaning her head on a shoulder that is not there; a man on the same bench resting his cheek on hair that is not there. Each sends theirs privately, and the press takes both inks and prints nothing. On Saturday at nine the press runs. The sheets that only ever had one ink come out blank, and nobody ever knows. The one that had both comes out whole, and where they touch, a colour neither ink had.

Somebody who sees it once can explain celestual to a friend: it only prints if you both send one.

perspective
The press's. It sees both halves, which neither of them can, and prints only pairs: the double blind, drawn as a machine.
the world
Every frame a riso print on warm paper, in rose, ice and black. Twelve prints a second, each a hair out of register. The words are printed too.
the sound
A hocket: one tune split between two players, each half full of holes until both play. From nine, the press is the rhythm section.
the end
The registration mark in the sheet's corner slides into register and becomes the mark.

the hook, a hocket

Eighth notes, a bar each. iris plays the odd eighths on the marimba, jun the even ones on the vibraphone. Alone, each is a tune with holes in it. From bar 8 they play together and it is whole. First draft, tuned by ear in the first round.

the print rules

  • Paper #F4F0E4 and three inks: rose #DF93AF for her, ice #8FB8DC for him, black for the world and the words. Rose over ice, about #8371A9, only where they touch.
  • Inks are filters: nothing is lighter than paper, so a light is paper left bare in a field of ink (the moon, the scanner's bar).
  • Above 78 percent the dots join and print solid, with the drum's mottle. A dropout now and then.
  • The camera is a copy stand: straight down, sheets slid in and out on the beat. A cut is a new sheet; nothing dissolves.
  • Every sheet is cut to 9 by 16, the shape of a phone's glass.
  • Never a halftone filter over a render, a grunge pack, CMYK, or anything "vintage".

what could go wrong

script B

pacific

32 s · 1080 × 1920 · 30 fps from 60 · 90 bpm · E major · twelve bars

it's already tomorrow where they are.

Theo is in Berkeley. Mina went home to Seoul, sixteen hours ahead, so when it is night for him it is the next afternoon for her: they live on different days now. Each still keeps the other's city on their phone, so the weather is the one thing they still share: on his phone at night, Seoul's sun; on hers in the afternoon, Berkeley's fog. Each writes a note and sends it privately. On Saturday at 9pm Pacific, which is 1pm on Sunday in Seoul, both phones turn rose on the same frame, and the rose runs through both worlds until the line between their days is gone.

The notes say the same thing from either side of the ocean: i still check the weather in seoul. and i still check the weather in berkeley.

perspective
The planet's. A camera that can be in two places at once, which is what the reveal is.
the world
Berkeley in ice, Seoul in amber, the world dithered the way the product draws a face. The phones crisp and lit, each glowing in the other's colour.
the sound
A phase piece: one figure, a beat apart on two sides of the ocean, creeping together until they land on each other at nine. A fog horn, a magpie.
the end
The two moons become one, and the line between their days bends into the mark's ring.

what could go wrong

The time difference is half the idea: five strangers, sound off, asked where each of them is and what time it is there. Seoul turned over could read as a mistake: test it against both the right way up (the toggle above). Long distance is a narrower story than a crush on campus.

which one

make two inks first.

It explains the mechanism in a picture anybody gets in one viewing, half a picture and then the whole, and says it again in sound.

Every element in it is something the product already owns: the riso, the bench telling, the rose and the ice, the mark's geometry. It reads as celestual and could not be anybody else's. It is the cheaper of the two to finish to the bar, and its 15 second cut is a whole film.

Pacific is the bigger swing, and the truest picture of the one thing the product does that nothing else does: it tells two people at the same moment, wherever each of them is. It is the film for the people far apart, and the second one to make. Both use the same two bodies and the same studio, so making one makes the other cheaper.
