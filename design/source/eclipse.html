A band that varies, honestly

A tilted ring projects both its edges by the same cosine, so the band is full width at the ends and foreshortened where it crosses the star. An earlier version added the width to the short axis un-scaled — 4.5× too fat at exactly the crossing — which is what made the ring read as a ribbon lying on the mark.

Drawn honestly it already varies two to one. Pushing the inner edge to the far side and turning it a little takes it to three to one: 6.4 at the ends, 4.4 near, 2.0 far.

Still the same star, drawn slim

starPath at equal arms and thick 1 redraws SPARK byte for byte — asserted on every change, so the mark is provably built on the curve the rest of the wall already uses.

It ships at 0.80, which draws the arms thinner without moving a single point. Four vertices, four cubics, one per concave sweep, and no corner anywhere in it.

Two limits, both checked

The inner edge must stay inside the outer or the band breaks open — past roughly bias 2 or twist 10 it does. And every arm must finish clear of the band: a side arm inside the hole, a vertical arm out past the outer edge. An arm that ends inside the band gets notched off and left as a floating tip.

On the shipped constants: edges closest at 0.075, side arms at 0.55 of the hole, vertical arms at 3.86 of the outer edge.
